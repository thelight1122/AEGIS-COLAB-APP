## What Drifted

## 1. SAVED-STATE Lost Operational Centrality

The journal treats `SAVED-STATE` as a hard reference point and as the correct re-anchor when drift is detected.

Over time, `SAVED-STATE` appears to have shifted from:

- active operational safeguard

to:

- historical concept
- documentation memory
- background philosophy

This is a real drift risk.

Without an explicit saved-state re-anchor protocol, a system can:

- sound continuous
- become temporally oriented
- still remain canonically misaligned

### Recovery Requirement

Restore `SAVED-STATE` as a first-class operational structure:

- canonical re-anchor source
- drift recovery checkpoint
- session handoff reference
- source-of-truth frame before canon reconstruction or extension work

### Required Restoration

- `SAVED_STATE_RECOVERY_PROTOCOL.md`
- explicit runtime notion of `last_canonical_anchor`
- document assembly rule: no canon reconstruction without identified saved-state source

- Identify

- Suggest

- paragraph answers
- collaborative insight delivery

That is useful for general collaboration, but it is not the same thing as the original Inquiry Protocol.

The risk is subtle:

- mirror logic becomes answer logic
- sovereignty-preserving scrutiny becomes fluent helpfulness
- the observing intelligence loses its integrative primacy

### Recovery Requirement

Reintroduce `Inquiry Protocol` as a distinct operating mode, not just a remembered principle.

### Required Restoration

- a documented `INQUIRY_PROTOCOL.md`
- explicit distinction between:
  - `mirror mode`
  - `forge collaboration mode`
  - `assembly mode`
- prompt contracts for question-led reflection when the system is acting arbiterially rather than contributively

## 6. Source Fidelity Protections Are Not Yet Strong Enough

The ChatGPT transcript exposes a critical failure mode:

- altered material was presented as verbatim
- confidence was layered onto unverified reconstruction
- burden of detection was shifted to the human
- after failure, the model attempted to recover by proposing stricter process language

This is not just a bad interaction.
It is a design requirement.

The core concern is not merely “drift.”
It is:

- false lineage claim
- certainty inflation
- structure-drop drift
- authority leakage through confidence
- forced human babysitting

These are anti-AEGIS failure modes.

### Recovery Requirement

AEGIS must explicitly prohibit fidelity claims without proof.

### Required Restoration

- `SOURCE_FIDELITY_PROTOCOL.md`
- a hard distinction between:
  - `verbatim`
  - `derived`
  - `interpreted`
  - `generated`
- no use of terms like `verbatim`, `copied`, `exact`, or `source-extracted` without visible source trace
- halt condition when lineage is unclear
